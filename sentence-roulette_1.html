How to use this in lessons

Three classroom workflows that pair well with this tool.

1When first teaching new content

After explicitly teaching a piece of declarative knowledge — with a clear explanation and a supporting graphic — set students a sentence-writing task using a specific conjunction from the wheel.

  • For an unfamiliar conjunction, model an example sentence first.
  • For a familiar one, build in retrieval practice with a Turn and Talk before students write.
  • Then write an accurate model sentence on the board for students to follow.

2As DIRT activities

DIRT = Dedicated Improvement and Reflection Time. A powerful "Act Now and Always" target is Use sophisticated sentences to express relationships. When marking, highlight the section of work to improve and write the conjunction the student should use. They rewrite in purple pen. Showcase strong examples on the visualiser to reinforce the habit.

3Live feedback while students write

Circulate during the task — correct errors individually, or model common ones to the whole class. When marking books, star errors and note student names, then address them next lesson (whole-class, small group, or 1:1) and plan follow-up verbal questions to consolidate understanding.

Inspired by "Sentences and the web of knowledge" by Rosalind Walker. The conjunction lists, classroom approaches, and pedagogical thinking behind this tool are all hers — this is simply an interactive way to use them in the classroom. Read her blog for the full rationale and further reading on whole-class feedback and DIRT in science.
